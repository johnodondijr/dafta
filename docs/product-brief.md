# Dafta Product Brief

## Vision

Dafta is a living notebook for notes, reminders, and the things people mean to
do. It helps users capture thoughts quickly, organize them intelligently, and
resurface the right thing at the right time.

## Target Users

- Busy professionals managing personal and work commitments.
- Creators and founders who capture ideas constantly.
- Students and researchers who need notes connected to action.
- ADHD-adjacent or easily overwhelmed users who need forgiving planning.
- Families or individuals managing recurring life admin.

## Market Insight

Most products in this category are strong in one direction and weak in another:

- Notes apps are good for thinking but weak at action.
- Task apps are good for execution but weak at rich context.
- Workspace apps are powerful but often heavy.
- Reminder apps notify users but rarely understand when a reminder is useful.

Dafta should sit in the gap: a calm memory and action system.

## Key Jobs To Be Done

- Capture a thought before it disappears.
- Turn a messy note into clear next steps.
- Remember commitments made to people.
- See what matters today without rebuilding a plan from scratch.
- Find old context without remembering where it was stored.
- Get reminded at a useful moment, not only at an arbitrary time.

## Feature Pillars

### Capture

- Quick text capture
- Voice capture
- Image and attachment capture
- Web clipping
- Email-to-Dafta capture
- Global command bar

### Remember

- Time reminders
- Location reminders
- Recurring reminders
- Calendar-aware reminders
- Multiple reminder nudges
- Open-loop follow-ups

### Organize

- Notebooks or spaces
- Projects
- Tags
- People
- Smart collections
- Linked notes

### Act

- Daily planning
- Top priorities
- Focus sessions
- Checklists
- Task dependencies
- Completion history

### Reflect

- Weekly review
- Overdue loops
- Notes worth revisiting
- Project health
- Habit and focus summaries

## Design Tone

Dafta should feel:

- Calm
- Premium
- Fast
- Warm
- Trustworthy
- Intelligent, but not loud

It should avoid feeling like a corporate dashboard, a generic kanban board, or
a maximalist productivity cockpit.

## Open Questions

- Should the first version be mobile-first, desktop-first, or web-first?
- Should Dafta be local-first from day one?
- Should collaboration be part of v1 or come later?
- Which UI inspirations should define the first visual direction?
- What level of AI should be included in the first prototype?
